  • 2.1Examples: Fibonacci and Predator–PreyMany processes can only be described with several quantities at once: young and adult pairs of rabbits, prey and predators. Each quantity gets its own recursion rule, and the rules depend on each other. This gives a system of difference equations.3 figs. · Apps
  • 2.2Matrix ModelsIf all recursion rules are linear, the system is written compactly as xn+1 = A xn with a matrix A and the state vector xn. One step is then a matrix–vector multiplication. Examples are a disease progression with three groups and the Leslie model for age classes.2 figs. · Apps
  • 2.3Eigenvalues and Long-Term BehaviorThe solution xn = An x0 can be decomposed with eigenvalues and eigenvectors. In the long run the dominant eigenvalue λ1, the one of largest modulus, prevails: it determines whether the system grows, shrinks, or comes to rest, and its eigenvector gives the distribution among the components.4 figs. · Apps
  • 2.4Nonlinear Systems: SIR ModelIn the SIR model a population is divided into susceptible (S), infected (I), and recovered (R) persons. New infections arise from contacts between S and I, hence the product β Sn In: the system is nonlinear. Whether a disease persists is decided by the basic reproduction number R0.4 figs. · Apps
  • 2.5In Depth: Propagation of the Action PotentialWith two variables per cell, the Chialvo model describes an action potential. If many such cells are coupled on a ring or in a plane, the excitation spreads as a wave. A badly timed second stimulation or a brief block can trigger a circulating excitation, a model for reentry arrhythmias.6 figs.
