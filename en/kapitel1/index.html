  • 1.1Sequences and Difference EquationsSequences describe quantities observed at fixed time steps: the length of a fish year by year, the number of malaria parasites day by day, pairs of rabbits month by month. A sequence can be given by a formula for the n-th term or by a recursion rule that computes each term from its predecessor.5 figs. · Apps
  • 1.2Linear Difference EquationsIn arithmetic sequences the same amount is added at each step, in geometric sequences the terms are multiplied by the same factor. Both are special cases of the linear difference equation xn+1 = a xn + b, which also includes Newton's law of cooling. With a varying inflow bn it describes the level of a drug.4 figs. · Apps
  • 1.3Logistic Growth ModelA population cannot grow without limit. In the logistic model the growth rate decreases with the population size and becomes zero at the carrying capacity K. After a change of variables the model depends on a single parameter a only, and already this simple equation shows cycles and chaos.7 figs. · Apps
  • 1.4Steady States and StabilitySteady states are the intersections of the update function with the diagonal. Whether a sequence runs toward a steady state or away from it is decided by the slope: for |f′(x*)| < 1 it is stable, for |f′(x*)| > 1 unstable. Periodic points are found as fixed points of fk.7 figs. · Apps
  • 1.5Newton's MethodMany equations cannot be solved for x. Newton's method replaces the function by its tangent and takes the root of the tangent as the next approximation. It is itself a difference equation and, when it converges, converges very fast.4 figs. · Apps
  • 1.6In Depth: Heart ModelsTwo simple difference equations describe heart rhythms beat by beat: the conduction in the AV node and the duration of action potentials under periodic stimulation. Both show how regular stimuli can produce irregular responses.6 figs. · Apps
