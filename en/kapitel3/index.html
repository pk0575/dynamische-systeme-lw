  • 3.1Differential Equations and Initial Value ProblemsLetting the time step of a difference equation tend to zero gives a differential equation: it describes the instantaneous rate of change dx/dt of a quantity. Together with an initial value it forms an initial value problem. Its solution is a function x(t) whose graph follows the slope of the direction field at every point.5 figs. · Apps
  • 3.2Exponential and Logistic Growthdx/dt = r x is the simplest growth model: the rate of change is proportional to the quantity itself, and the solution x(t) = x0 ert grows or decays exponentially. Limited resources lead to the logistic model dx/dt = r (1 − x/K) x, whose solutions approach the carrying capacity K.5 figs.
  • 3.3Steady States and StabilityAn autonomous differential equation dx/dt = f(x) is steady where f(x*) = 0. Whether the solutions run toward x* or away from it is shown by the phase line, and computationally by the sign of f′(x*). If f depends on a parameter, the bifurcation diagram describes how the steady states change – for instance when a stock is fished or in the gene switch of the lac operon.5 figs. · Apps
  • 3.4Euler's MethodMost differential equations cannot be solved in closed form. Euler's method replaces the solution by short line segments with the slope of the direction field: xn+1 = xn + h f(tn, xn). Smaller step sizes h give more accurate approximations; step sizes that are too large can make the method unstable.4 figs. · Apps
  • 3.5In Depth: Excitability of Heart CellsThe Mitchell–Schaeffer model describes the action potential of a cardiac muscle cell with a differential equation for the membrane potential v. If the channel variable h is held fixed, it has a resting state, a threshold, and an excited state. If h is allowed to change slowly, a complete action potential arises, and its duration and the restitution curve can be computed. The programs use Euler's method with h = 0.01 ms.3 figs.
